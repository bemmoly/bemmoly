The expense report that writes itself

Bemmoly Expenses is a product of its own: use it alone, or alongside Bemmoly's other products. Take a photo of a receipt, forward an email or tap a card, and the expense files itself, already coded and already checked against policy. Managers approve the safe ones in one go. Finance closes the month without chasing anyone. It's open source, it runs on your own server, and your company shapes every rule.

01The problem today

Expense reports are the most hated form at work, and most of the work in them is copying. The data already exists on the receipt, the card feed, the calendar and the travel booking. People still type it in by hand, and someone else checks it by hand.

37%of travel managers say attaching receipts is the worst stepGBTA Foundation, 2015
Receipts get lost before they are filed

People keep paper in wallets and PDFs in inboxes, then rebuild the month from memory. The worst steps are attaching receipts and typing in the data.

Policy is checked after the money is spent

The policy is a PDF nobody reads. People find out they broke a rule when finance rejects the claim three weeks later, and that feels like a punishment.

Managers rubber-stamp, or sit on it

An approver sees 40 lines with no context. They either approve all of them blind or let the queue sit, and reimbursements wait on whoever is slowest.

Finance does the real review by hand

Duplicates, split bills, weekend claims and round numbers get caught by eye in spreadsheets. Most errors are found late, and most claims that are fine still get checked line by line.

Tools are priced per seat and locked to a card

The best experiences come bundled with a corporate card or a travel agency, and they are priced per person per month. Small teams and companies outside the US get the leftovers.

Every company's rules are different

Per diems by city, mileage rates by vehicle, GST input credit, project codes and cost centres all differ. Rigid tools force the company to bend its policy to the software.

Best "zero-touch" feel. Most expenses never need a form.
The full experience needs its card. Out-of-pocket claims and non-US use come second.
BrexCard-led · global

Global cards and spend limits per budget, with policies attached to each budget, an AI assistant that answers spenders, and multi-entity support.

Budget-first thinking: you spend from a budget, not just against a policy.

04Where Bemmoly does better

We take the leaders' best ideas (zero-touch capture, budget thinking, the trip as a unit) and add what only an open, self-hosted platform can do.

The expense is the unit, not the report

Each receipt is a live item that files, checks and routes itself. Reports exist only where a company wants them (a trip, a month). Nobody "builds a report" again.

Leaders: card-led tools do this; reporting-led tools still make reports the job.
Policy you can read, change and test

Rules are written as sentences ("Meals: up to ₹2,000 per person"). Each rule is versioned and can be tested against last quarter's claims before it goes live, so finance sees the impact first.

New: nobody lets you replay a policy change against history.
Any card, any bank, or no card at all

Card feeds come in through connectors (a bank file, an open banking API, a card issuer). Out-of-pocket claims get the same treatment as card spend. You keep your bank.

Leaders: the best experience needs their card.
It already knows the context

Bemmoly already knows the org chart, the projects, time off and calendars. A claim knows who approves it, which project it belongs to, and that you were in Berlin that week.

New: standalone tools have to re-import all of this.
AI on your terms

Receipt reading, coding and risk notes run on the model you choose, including a local one. With AI off, capture still works through plain OCR rules and nothing lilac appears.

New: every leader sends receipts to their own cloud AI.
No seat price, ever

Every person in the company can file claims, from interns to contractors. No "approver licence", no paid AI, no premium policy tier.

Leaders: per person per month, with premium tiers for policy and multi-entity.

Six screens, drawn in the Expenses app's own shell with Bemmoly's tokens. Click the tabs, or click the highlighted controls inside a screen to follow the flow. The appearance switch at the top right flips every screen between light and dark.

06Make it yours

Bemmoly is open source and used by companies of every shape, so nothing about expenses is hard-coded. A company changes five things, all from Expenses › Settings, without writing code. Each can also be exported as a file, so it can be reviewed like code.

Categories

The company's own list, nested ("Travel › Flights"), each with a GL account, tax treatment, receipt rule and icon. Starts from a template: India, EU, US, or a blank list.

Policy rules

Sentences built from blocks: when, limit, then. Limits per person, day, trip, city tier or grade. Every edit is a new version with a dry run against history.

Approval flows

Built on Bemmoly's shared workflow engine: by amount, category, project or cost centre. Delegates during time off come from People automatically.

Fields

Custom fields per category (a client name for client meals, a vehicle for mileage), stored typed and filterable, usable in rules and exports.

Money

Base currency per entity, exchange-rate source, mileage rates per vehicle, per diem tables by city, and reimbursement methods (bank file, payroll, or a payment connector).

Connectors

Card feeds, the ledger (Tally, Zoho Books, QuickBooks, Xero, NetSuite or a CSV mapping), and travel bookings. Each one is a plug-in with a public interface, so the community can add more.

08How it connects

Expenses is its own product with its own app, and a company can run it alone. When other Bemmoly products are installed, they share one sign-in, one person directory and one approval engine through the kernel, so each one gets better with the others.

With other Bemmoly products

  • People: managers, cost centres, grades, entities, bank details, and delegates during time off. Without People, Expenses keeps its own simple approver list.
  • Work: projects and clients as expense projects, and billable spend on the project
  • Time: billable hours and billable expenses go into one client invoice
  • Licences: recurring software spend becomes tracked applications ("Add to Licences" opens Licences)
  • Helpdesk: "Where's my reimbursement?" is answered from the tracker

Core objects

  • expense: amount, currency, date, merchant, category, fields, attachments, source (camera, email, chat, card), state
  • trip: a grouping with dates, places and per diem results
  • card_txn: from a feed, matched to an expense
  • policy and policy_version: rules as data, with dry-run results
  • payout_batch: a reimbursement run and its export

10Open questions

  1. Do we issue cards, or only read feeds?Recommendation: read feeds only. Issuing cards makes us a financial product, which conflicts with "no in-app purchases".
  2. Which ledgers come first?Recommendation: CSV mapping, then Tally and Zoho Books for India, and Xero for everyone else, from where the first users are.
  3. Reimbursement rails?Recommendation: a bank bulk-payment file plus a "paid through payroll" mark. Payment APIs come as community connectors.
  4. Is the receipt address one shared inbox or one per person?Recommendation: one shared address that matches by the sender's verified email, plus plus-addressing for delegates.
