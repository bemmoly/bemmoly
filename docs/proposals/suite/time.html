The timesheet that fills itself, and stays yours

Your calendar, the issues you moved, the docs you edited and the code you reviewed already say where the week went. Time drafts the week from that, you confirm it in a minute, and your manager approves the clean ones in one go. It never takes a screenshot or counts a keystroke. 01The problem today

Timesheets ask people to rebuild their week from memory on Friday afternoon. The record already exists in calendars and work tools, but nobody uses it, so time leaks out of invoices, budgets are guessed, and some companies answer the distrust with surveillance.

~15%of chargeable consulting work is never billed to the clientLondon Business School and TIQ, 2016
57%of time in Microsoft 365 goes to meetings, chat and email, the work timesheets miss mostMicrosoft Work Trend Index, 2023
44%of business owners say timesheet errors are their biggest time tracking struggleQuickBooks Time survey of 731 owners, 2018
60%of large employers use tools to track their workers, double the pre-pandemic levelGartner, 2022
The week is rebuilt from memory

Most people fill timesheets once or twice a week, or less. By Friday, Tuesday is a blur, so entries get rounded, guessed or copied from last week.

40% record once or twice a week, 22% once or twice a month (LBS and TIQ, 2016)
Meetings, email and reviews vanish

The small things are the first to go. In one study 58% of advisors logged less than a fifth of their email time and half logged under a fifth of client calls.

LBS and TIQ, 2016
Forgetting is the most common error

Missed clock-ins and unlogged hours lead the list of timesheet errors, and most companies don't review sheets before payroll, so mistakes surface as pay disputes.

34% name forgetting as the top cause; 59% require no review (QuickBooks Time, 2018)
Distrust turns into surveillance

The market's answer to bad data has been screenshots every few minutes, activity percentages and app tracking. People feel watched, and the hours still don't say what the work was.

Gartner: 40% of employees were told nothing about what data is collected (2022)
Desk and shift teams use different tools

Engineers log project time in one product, support and lab staff clock in on another, and HR reconciles both against time off by hand. Labour law keeps raising the bar: the EU's top court ruled in 2019 that working time must be recorded objectively.

CJEU, CCOO v Deutsche Bank, C-55/18, 14 May 2019
Budgets are read after they burn

Project leads find out a fixed-fee phase is over budget when finance runs the invoice. Billable and non-billable time sit in different reports, and rework never gets a name.

03Market leaders, and what they teach us

The category splits four ways: simple trackers with timers, automatic trackers that watch your desktop, tools native to a work tracker, and attendance apps for shift teams. This is an internal benchmark. Product screens never name these products.

HarvestSimple tracking · agencies

A timer, day and week timesheets, approvals, budgets that update as people log, and invoices made from tracked time and expenses.

Time to invoice in one product, with budgets that move live.
Everything still starts with a person pressing start or typing hours.
Toggl Track and ClockifyTimers for everyone

Fast timers everywhere (browser, desktop, phone), calendar events beside entries, idle detection, approvals, billable rates and reports. Clockify adds a PIN kiosk and GPS for field staff.

Toggl's private Timeline: you see your own app history and choose what becomes an entry.
Optional activity and location monitoring sit one setting away from the respectful defaults.
TimelyAI auto-tracking

A desktop "Memory" app records time across apps and sites privately, and AI drafts timesheet entries, assigns projects and lets people approve them in one click.

The closest to the dream, and explicit about privacy: no screenshots, no keystrokes, only approved entries are shared.
Needs a desktop agent watching app usage, and the AI runs in its cloud.
Tempo TimesheetsNative to Jira

Worklogs live on issues. Calendar, IDE and Git activity becomes suggested entries, team leads approve, approved periods lock, and accounts split billable from CapEx and OpEx.

Time attached to the work item, which makes reporting by epic and account natural.
Lives inside one tracker. Time off, attendance and payroll are somebody else's problem.
HubstaffMonitoring-led

Time tracking with activity levels per 10-minute block from keyboard and mouse input, optional screenshots up to three per monitor every 10 minutes, and app and URL tracking.

Strong payroll and field features for distributed teams paid by the hour.
Treats presence as productivity. Its own docs warn against scoring people on activity rates.
Deputy and When I WorkShifts and attendance

Shift scheduling, clock-in on a phone or a shared tablet with a location stamp or photo, break rules, overtime tracking and one-tap export to payroll.

Built for people who don't sit at a desk: big buttons, shift swaps, breaks done right.
No idea of projects or billable work, so mixed teams need a second product.

04Where Bemmoly does better

We take the leaders' best ideas (time on the work item, the private timeline, invoices from hours, attendance done right on a phone) and add what an open, self-hosted kernel makes possible.

The week drafts itself from the work

Calendar events, issues moved, docs edited, reviews given and tickets solved become a drafted week. Each number opens to the evidence, and the person confirms. No desktop agent, no app history.

vs Timely and Replicon: drafts from desktop and app activity, in their cloud.
Respect is a rule, not a setting

No screenshots, no keystroke or mouse counts, no app or URL tracking, no idle shaming. Not as defaults, and not as options an admin can turn on. Time records work, not people.

vs Hubstaff: screenshots up to three per monitor every 10 minutes, and activity percentages.
Signals belong to the person

The raw signals behind a draft are visible only to the person whose week it is. Managers see confirmed entries and rule flags, never what someone opened or when they were idle.

New: monitoring tools show managers raw activity by default.
Time off and holidays are already there

Leave and holiday calendars come from People, so a half day off fills itself and minimum hours pro-rate. Without People, Time keeps its own simple holiday list.

vs Tempo and Harvest: time off comes from a plug-in or an import.
Project time and attendance, per team

Engineers log project time, the support desk clocks in on shifts, the lab does both. One product, one set of rules, one approval route, one payroll export.

vs Deputy and When I Work: shifts only. vs simple trackers: projects only.
Budgets, invoices, and no seat price

Approved billable hours and billable expenses become one client invoice, budgets forecast their own end, and every person in the company can log time at no cost.

vs every leader: priced per person, with approvals or AI in paid tiers.

06Make it yours

Bemmoly is open source and used by companies of every shape, so nothing about time is hard-coded. A company sets six things in Time › Settings, without writing code. Each can be exported as a file and reviewed like code.

Projects and time codes

Projects (from Work, or Time's own), clients, and a list of time codes such as Development, Code review, Client meetings, On call, Rework. Each code carries billable, CapEx or OpEx, and a ledger account.

Rules

Sentences built from blocks: rounding per client, minimum hours pro-rated for time off, overtime by location, rest between days, required notes, future-time limits, and lock periods.

Modes per team

Project time, attendance, or both. Weekly or daily sheets. Drafting on or off. Engineers, support shifts and a lab can each run the way they work, under one product.

Approval routes

Built on Bemmoly's shared workflow engine: manager, then project owner for billable time, then People ops for overtime. Delegates during time off come from People.

Holidays and time off

Holiday calendars per office (Karnataka, Berlin, Texas) and approved leave come from People and fill themselves in. Without People, Time keeps its own holiday list.

Signal sources

Connectors for calendars, Git hosts, Work, Docs and Helpdesk, each a plug-in with a public interface. A company picks which ones may feed drafts; each person can turn any off for themselves.

07AI, with a person in charge

Privacy. Signals are work objects, never activity: no screenshots, keystrokes, mouse movement, app or website history, and no chat or email content. Signals are visible only to the person, kept 30 days, and deleted on request. Managers never see signals, only confirmed entries. 08How it connects

Time is its own product with its own app, and a company can run it alone. When other Bemmoly products are installed, they share one sign-in, one person directory and one approval engine through the kernel, so each one gets better with the others. Every cross-product action asks the person first and has a fallback.

With other Bemmoly products

  • People: managers, teams, offices, contracted hours, holiday calendars and approved time off. Without People, Time keeps its own simple team list and holidays.
  • Work: projects, issues and their moves become signals, and a timer can start on an issue. Without Work, Time keeps its own projects.
  • Docs: doc edits become signals; "Draft a change request" opens a Docs draft, or downloads a summary without Docs.
  • Expenses: billable expenses join billable hours on one client invoice.
  • Helpdesk: tickets solved and on-call pages become signals for support and on-call codes.
  • Performance: deliberately not connected. Hours are not a measure of performance.

Core objects

  • time_entry: person, day, start and end or hours, project, time code, note, source (timer, draft, quick entry, clock), state
  • timesheet: a person's period, its rules result and approval state
  • signal: a private, short-lived pointer to a work object, owned by the person
  • time_code and rule_set: billing, booking and rules as data, versioned
  • shift and clock_event: attendance mode
  • project_budget and invoice_run: budgets, forecasts and exports

10Open questions

  1. How long do signals live, and who can see them?Recommendation: only the person, for 30 days, then deleted. Admins can shorten it but never lengthen it or read them.
  2. Should managers see how well a sheet matched its signals?Recommendation: no. A match score turns signals into surveillance by the back door. Managers see rule flags only.
  3. Does attendance mode need to come earlier for EU customers?Recommendation: ship a basic clock-in and break record with the first release in the EU, since recording working time is expected there; the full shift mode stays later.
  4. Do we build rostering, or only attendance?Recommendation: attendance and simple shift swaps in Time; full rostering and demand planning as a community connector.
  5. Does Time calculate pay?Recommendation: no. It exports approved hours with overtime, night and on-call buckets; payroll systems calculate pay.
