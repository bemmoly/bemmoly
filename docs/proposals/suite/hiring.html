01The problem today

Hiring is where a company makes its first promise to a person, and most companies break it. Candidates wait in silence. Recruiters drown in applications, many written by AI. Interviews drift into conversations about whether someone "feels right", and the decision is made by whoever speaks first in the debrief.

Candidates are left in silence

Most people who apply never hear a clear no. Even after an interview, a reply depends on one busy recruiter remembering to send it. The company's reputation is built in that silence.

Greenhouse, 2024
Volume has outrun people

Mass applying with AI tools means hundreds of applications per role. Reviewers skim in seconds, and the easiest filters (a school, a company name, a gap) are the least fair ones.

Greenhouse, 2024 · Gartner, 2025
Interviews without structure

Each interviewer asks what they like and writes "great energy" in the feedback. Structured interviews predict job performance far better, yet most teams still run them by feel.

Sackett et al., 2022
The debrief is decided by the loudest voice

Feedback is often written after the debrief, or after reading a colleague's strong opinion. Independent judgement disappears, and "culture fit" stands in for evidence.

AI that judges people nobody can question

Black-box match scores rank candidates with no explanation. Candidates don't trust it, regulators now treat hiring AI as high risk (the EU AI Act, New York City's bias-audit law), and the company carries the liability.

Gartner, 2025 · EU AI Act · NYC DCWP
The hire falls between tools

The offer is a document, approvals are emails, and onboarding starts again in the HR system from a copy-paste. A good hire can arrive on day one to no laptop and no accounts. Each hire already costs thousands (SHRM put the average near $4,700 in 2022).

SHRM, 2022

03Market leaders, and what they teach us

Applicant tracking has split into structured-hiring suites, all-in-one analytics platforms, brand-led tools for smaller teams, enterprise suites inside HR systems, and sourcing tools that feed them. This is an internal benchmark. Product screens never name these products.

GreenhouseStructured hiring

Popularised structured hiring: scorecards with a few attributes per category, interview kits per stage, and a careful candidate experience. Added identity checks against fake applicants in 2025.

The method: interview plans, kits and scorecards as the backbone of every job.
Priced for mid-size and larger companies. Candidate communication still depends on people remembering.
AshbyAll-in-one, analytics

ATS, CRM, scheduling and deep analytics in one. Its AI-assisted application review checks CVs against criteria with citations, redacts personal details first, and never ranks.

The most careful AI design in the market: criteria, citations, opt-out, third-party audit.
Dense for occasional users. Cloud only, so CVs leave the company.
Lever and GemCRM and sourcing

Lever (now part of Employ, with Jobvite and JazzHR) joined ATS and candidate CRM. Gem started as sourcing and outreach, and now sells an all-in-one platform with a large profile database.

Treating candidates as relationships over years, not single applications.
Outreach automation can feel like spam to the people it reaches.
TeamtailorEmployer brand

A no-code careers site builder with employee stories, talent pools and an opt-in AI co-pilot whose modules can be switched on per team.

Careers pages that look like the company, made by recruiters without IT.
Lighter on structured evaluation and on integrations.
Workable and Zoho RecruitSmall and mid-size

Fast setup, job board posting, interview self-scheduling from calendars, and AI sourcing that suggests passive candidates from large databases.

Self-scheduling and one-click posting: time to first interview drops.
Per-job or per-seat pricing. AI match scores rank people by similarity to a job description.
SmartRecruiters and iCIMSEnterprise

High-volume hiring, conversational AI, CRM and compliance at scale. SmartRecruiters became part of SAP in September 2025, alongside SuccessFactors.

Covers every country, every approval and every audit trail.
Long rollouts, heavy screens, and a growing pull into one vendor's HR suite.

04Where Bemmoly does better

We take the leaders' best ideas (structured kits, criteria-based AI with citations, careers pages recruiters can build) and add what only an open, self-hosted platform with one person record can do.

A reply-by promise, kept by the system

Each stage has a reply-by time (say, five working days). The candidate sees the date on their own page. The team gets nudged before it slips, and if nobody acts, a kind holding note goes out on time.

New: leaders offer templates and alerts, nobody makes the promise visible to the candidate.
Fair by construction, not by training

Names, photos, addresses and school names are hidden at first review. Interviewers write feedback before they see anyone else's. Job posts and feedback get a language check. All on by default.

Leaders: blind review is partial or an add-on; open source has none.
Evidence, not vibes

Must-haves are set when the role opens and reused everywhere: the CV check, the interview kit, the scorecard and the debrief grid. Competencies come from the same library Performance uses after the hire.

New: hiring and performance on one competency model.
The hire is already a person

When the offer is accepted, the kernel's person record is created and, where installed, People starts onboarding, Assets orders the laptop and Access prepares accounts for the start date.

Leaders: an integration to another vendor, and a re-typed record.
AI that shows evidence and never judges

AI finds evidence for each must-have in a CV and cites the line. It never scores, ranks or rejects anyone. Personal details are removed first, the model is the company's choice (local included), and applicants can opt out.

Leaders: most sell match scores; the careful ones still send CVs to their cloud.
Every interviewer, no seat price

Hiring managers, interviewers and approvers all take part with no licence. Retention, consent and deletion rules per country are part of the product, not a compliance add-on.

Leaders: priced per employee or per job, with compliance in higher tiers.

05The experience

Eight screens, drawn in Bemmoly Hiring's own shell and tokens. Click the tabs, or the highlighted controls inside a screen to follow the flow. The appearance switch at the top right flips every screen between light and dark.

06Make it yours

Every company hires differently: a bakery hiring seasonal staff, a bank hiring under regulators, a start-up hiring its first engineers. Nothing is hard-coded. Six things change from Hiring › Settings, without code, and each can be exported as a file so it can be reviewed like code.

Stages and promises

Process templates per kind of role ("Engineering, experienced", "Hourly, stores"). Each stage has a type, an owner, a reply-by time, whether names are hidden, and the email it sends. Jobs start from a template and can adjust it.

Scorecards and competencies

One competency library shared with Performance. Scorecard templates pick competencies per role and level, with questions, follow-ups and "what good looks like" for each, and a rating scale the company names.

Approval chains

Requisitions and offers run on the shared workflow engine. Steps by rule: above the band midpoint, over a total, a new headcount, a role in a regulated team. Delegates on leave come from the person directory.

Careers page and brand

A page builder with the company's logo, colours, fonts, team stories and benefits, served from the company's own domain. Job posts, the application form and the candidate status page all wear the brand.

Email templates

Every candidate email is a template with fields, per language and per stage: invite, holding note, decline, offer. A tone check flags cold or legalistic wording. Candidate-facing words are reviewed once, not written each time.

Privacy and retention

Per country: legal basis, how long applications are kept, talent pool consent, which voluntary questions may be asked, whether AI may read CVs, and how deletion requests run. Typed custom fields on jobs and applications.

07AI, with a person in charge

Hiring is where AI can do the most harm, so the line is drawn hard. AI reads, organises and points out; it never scores, ranks, rejects or advances anyone, and it never infers protected traits. It does not analyse faces, voices or video. Lilac marks every AI surface, each one cites its sources, a person applies the result, and nothing appears while AI is off. The model is the company's choice, including a local one.

Privacy and law. Names, contact details, photos and other identifying details are removed before a CV reaches any model, and applicants can opt out on the form. Every AI output is logged with its inputs, so a company can explain any outcome. Hiring AI is high risk under the EU AI Act (Annex III, employment), and New York City requires a yearly bias audit for automated employment decision tools. Because Bemmoly's AI never makes or ranks the decision, and a person always reviews the evidence, it is designed to stay on the side of those rules. A company can still switch AI off per country.

08How it connects

Hiring is its own product. Use it alone, or with the others. Under the hood it shares Bemmoly's kernel: one sign-in, one person directory, the approval and workflow engine, typed custom fields, the AI layer, audit and backups. A cross-product action asks the person first, and still works when the other product isn't installed.

Other Bemmoly products

  • People: headcount plans open requisitions; an accepted offer creates the person record and starts onboarding. Without People, the kernel's people list gets the record and the hiring manager gets an onboarding checklist
  • Assets: a laptop request for the start date. Without it, a task for the IT owner
  • Access: the role's access bundle, live on day one. Without it, a task listing the accounts to create
  • Performance: one competency library for scorecards and reviews; the hiring scorecard becomes the first check-in's context, if the new person agrees
  • Work and Docs: a first-week plan as a Work project; job descriptions and kits can be drafted in Docs
  • Time: optional tracking of interview hours by team, for the cost of hiring

Core objects

  • job: role, team, location, pay range, must-haves, process template, approval state
  • candidate and application: one candidate, many applications over time, each with source, stage and reply-by date
  • interview and scorecard: kit, panel, ratings per competency, notes, submitted time
  • decision: outcome, reason, who decided
  • offer: terms, band check, approvals, letter, signature
  • consent: country rule, talent pool opt-in, AI opt-out, deletion date

10Open questions

  1. Should declined candidates get a reason?Recommendation: offer a short reason drawn from the scorecard competencies, reviewed by a person before sending, off by default and switched on per country after legal review.
  2. Do we build sourcing and a profile database?Recommendation: no. Receive candidates from professional networks and job boards through connectors. A scraped profile database conflicts with consent rules and with our privacy stance.
  3. How do we handle fake or AI-generated applicants?Recommendation: identity checks as an optional connector at a late stage, never an automatic rejection, and never stored images in Bemmoly.
  4. Should we collect diversity data at all?Recommendation: only where lawful, voluntary, in a separate store that reviewers can't see, aggregate only with small groups hidden. Off by default.
  5. What if a company wants AI to rank applicants?Recommendation: we don't build it. It's the part regulators treat as highest risk and candidates trust least. Evidence per must-have gives most of the speed without the harm.
