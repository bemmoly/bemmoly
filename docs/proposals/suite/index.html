Run the whole company on one open platform

Nine business products: people, time, money, things, software, hiring, access, growth and help. Each one is its own product with its own app, so a company can install one or all nine. Underneath, they share Bemmoly's kernel: one sign-in, one person record, one approval engine and one AI layer. Each is as good as the best product in its category, free forever, on your own server, and shaped by your company's rules rather than ours.

MIT, no open core Self-hosted, one app plus Postgres No seat price, no tiers AI optional, bring your own model

01Nine products

02One platform under all of them

Each product is separate on the surface: its own app, its own navigation, its own settings, and its own audience. The market also sells these as nine separate tools, but each of those re-imports the org chart, keeps its own approval rules and sends its own notifications. Bemmoly builds that plumbing once in the kernel, so every product starts with the context the others already have.

One person, one record

People owns the record. Every other product reads the same manager, team, location, cost centre and employment state, so a change made once (a transfer, a new manager) reaches approvals, access and assets the same minute.

One approval engine

Expense claims, timesheets, asset requests, access grants, offers and leave all run on one workflow engine. Admins learn one builder, delegates during time off work everywhere, and one notification stream tells a person what needs them across every product they use.

03One person, every product

This is where a suite on one platform beats nine best-in-class tools. Follow Kavya Nair, a frontend engineer who accepts an offer on 5 October and starts on Monday 2 November. Nobody copies her details from one system to another.

5 OctOffer signed

Hiring hands the accepted offer to People. Her record exists with role, level, salary band and start date.

6 OctOnboarding opens

People starts the onboarding journey for her role and location, and fans out tasks to every product below.

23 OctLaptop ships

Assets reserves the standard engineering kit, records the serials, and tracks the courier to her door.

2 Nov, 08:00Access is ready

Access grants birthright apps for Frontend in Bengaluru. Licences takes a seat from the pool rather than buying one.

2 NovGoals for 30, 60, 90

Performance drafts her first goals from the onboarding plan. Her manager, Daniel, edits and agrees them.

First weekTime and money ready

Her timesheet knows her projects. Expenses knows her approver, her card and her grade's limits.

Any dayHelp that knows her

When she asks "how do I get VPN access?", Helpdesk already knows her device, her apps and her team.

And in reverse. When someone leaves, one exit event in People does the rest: access is revoked at the end of the last day, licences go back to the pool, Assets opens the return with a courier label, the final expense claims and timesheets are flagged for closure, and Helpdesk hands their open tickets to their manager. Each step is logged, and nothing depends on someone remembering a checklist.

04Separate products, one front door

Each product opens on its own. Most people use only two or three, though, so every person also gets a start page after sign-in. It shows the products they can use, what's waiting on them in each, and one question box that works across all of them. It isn't a tenth product. It's the doorway, and the product switcher in every app returns to it.

05Each product, its own app

Every product has its own shell, built the way Pipelines' is: its brand block, its own places, its own search, its own settings, and a switcher (the grid button) to move between products. The pattern is the same in all nine, so moving between them feels familiar, but none of them carries another's navigation.

Separate where people see it

  • Each product is its own web app, served under its own path (/expenses, /helpdesk) on the same origin, as ADR 0037 set up for Pipelines. A product can get its own host later.
  • Its own shell, navigation, search, settings, notifications and phone layout
  • Its own grant: an admin turns a product on and says who may use it. Nobody sees a product they can't use.
  • Install one product, or all nine. Expenses works without People; it keeps a simple approver list until People is there.

Shared where nobody sees it

  • One sign-in and single sign-on across products, so the switcher never asks for a password again
  • One person record: People owns it when installed, and the kernel's people list otherwise
  • One approval and workflow engine, one custom-field system, one audit log, one backup
  • One AI setting: the workspace picks the model once, and every product uses it

06Make it yours: five levers, every product

Open source means companies of every shape, size and country will use this. Each product has its own settings, and every product's settings are built from the same five levers. An admin who has set up Expenses already knows how to set up Assets. Every lever can be exported as a file, so a company can review it, version it in Git, or share a template with others.

Fields

Typed custom fields on any object: a vehicle on a mileage claim, a T-shirt size on a new hire, a cost centre on a licence.

field: vehicle type: choice on: expense.mileage
Types and categories

The company's own lists: expense categories, asset types, ticket categories, leave types, job families. Each carries its own fields and rules.

category: IT › Laptop form: laptop_issue team: it-support
Policies as rules

Rules written as sentences and stored as data: limits, eligibility, accrual, SLAs, reclaim rules. Versioned, with a dry run against history.

when: meals.client limit: 1500/attendee over: ask_reason
Workflows

One engine for approvals and lifecycles: by amount, sensitivity, team or location, with delegates and escalations.

step: manager if: amount > 10000 then: cost_centre_owner
Connectors

Banks, ledgers, payroll, device management, directories, chat. Each is a plug-in with a public interface, so the community can add more.

connector: tally direction: export map: categories

Templates to start from

Country packs (India with GST, PF and state holidays; Germany; the US) and company-size packs. A new workspace starts with a sane setup, not a blank form.

Roles that make sense

Capabilities per product (use, approve, administer, audit) and field visibility rules (who sees salary or bank details), all on Bemmoly's existing capability system.

Extend with code when needed

Every object has a typed API and webhooks, and connectors use the same plug-in interface as the products. A company that outgrows the levers writes a plug-in, without forking.

07AI across the suite

AI shows up where it removes copying, judgement-free checking or searching. It follows the same rules everywhere: it's optional, it's lilac, it cites, a person applies, and the workspace picks the model, including a local one for sensitive data.

09Open questions for Keerthi

  1. Same origin under a path, or a host per product?Recommendation: start under paths on one origin (/expenses), exactly like Pipelines, so sign-in and cookies work unchanged. Offer expenses.acme.dev later through the same ADR step Pipelines has queued.
  2. Payroll: build or connect?Recommendation: connect. Payroll is regulated per country and changes every year. People exposes a payroll export and a connector interface, and the community can maintain country packs.
  3. Names.Proposed: People, Time, Expenses, Assets, Licences, Hiring, Access, Performance, Helpdesk. Plain words, consistent with Work and Docs.
  4. Mobile.Capture (receipts, clock-in, approvals, asset scan) needs a phone. Recommendation: an installable web app first, with camera and offline queue, then native shells if needed.
  5. External use.Helpdesk for customers and Hiring's careers page face the public. They need public pages with their own rate limits and branding rules. Confirm that's in scope for the first versions.
