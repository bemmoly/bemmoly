Stop paying for software nobody opens

Bemmoly Licences is a product of its own: use it alone, or with Bemmoly's other products. It finds every app the company pays for from the card spend, invoices and sign-ins it can see, shows who actually uses each one, and gives back the seats nobody needs, with a polite nudge first. Before each renewal it hands you a brief: how use has moved, how many seats to keep, and the date you must give notice by. It's open source, it runs on your own server, and your company sets every rule.

01The problem today

Software is bought by every team, on every card, and renews on its own. Nobody holds the full list, so nobody can say which seats are idle until the invoice has already been paid for another year.

Nobody has the full list

Apps arrive on corporate cards, personal cards that get expensed, invoices to someone's inbox and free trials that turn paid. The list lives in a spreadsheet that was right last March.

Productiv found 51% of apps were still shadow IT in 2023.
Seats are bought for the peak and never given back

Teams buy for headcount plans and launches. When people move teams or stop using a tool, the seat stays assigned and billed, because nobody owns the job of taking it back.

Productiv: 53% of licences unused over 90 days, 2023.
Renewals are surprises

Contracts auto-renew, and notice periods of 30 to 90 days pass quietly. By the time anyone looks at usage, the only option left is to pay and promise to fix it next year.

Zylo 2025: 66.5% of IT leaders saw unexpected charges from usage-based or AI pricing.
Usage is locked inside each vendor's admin page

Each vendor shows its own last-active dates in its own console. Single sign-on shows sign-ins, not cost. Finance has cost, not use. Putting the three together is a monthly spreadsheet job.

Leavers keep costing money

Offboarding switches off the identity, but the seat is still on the invoice. The account sits deactivated and paid for until someone counts it at renewal.

People buy what the company already has

An employee who needs a diagramming tool puts one on their card, while the company has 70 idle seats of another. There's no catalogue to check first, and asking IT means a ticket and a wait.

04Where Bemmoly does better

We keep the leaders' best ideas (the spend-first inventory, reclaim as a workflow, a number before every renewal) and add what an open platform that already holds the expense, identity and people data can do.

Discovery from signals it already owns

With Expenses and Access installed, the card swipes, invoices and SSO sign-ins are already in the same kernel. No finance export to schedule, no identity sync to babysit. On its own, Licences reads the same signals through connectors.

Leaders: the same signals, pulled in through integrations you set up and keep alive.
Reclaim that asks first and finishes the job

Inactive people get a one-tap "keep it or release it" message. A week later the seat is freed through Access or the vendor's admin API, and goes back to the pool for the next person who asks.

Leaders: automation-led tools do this well; discovery-led tools often stop at the report.
A renewal brief that knows your plans

The brief joins the usage trend with open roles from Hiring, joiners and leavers from People, and the quote in the contract, so the seat number is grounded in what's coming, not just what happened.

Leaders: buying-led tools bring pooled price benchmarks. We can't and won't pool other companies' contracts; we bring your own plans.
Request an app, and get one you already own

People describe the need in plain words. Licences offers tools the company already pays for, with an idle seat granted on the spot. Only truly new apps go to security and finance.

Leaders: app catalogues exist, usually as a separate portal that people forget.
AI on your terms

Vendor matching, contract reading, briefs and request matching run on the model the workspace picks, including a local one. With AI off, rules and plain matching do the work and nothing lilac appears.

New: leaders run their AI in their own cloud, on your contracts.
No percentage of what you save

No per-employee price, no fee tied to spend under management, no paid tier for automation. The tool that cuts software costs doesn't become one.

Leaders: priced on request, usually by company size or managed spend.

06Make it yours

Every company buys software differently, so nothing here is hard-coded. A company sets six things in Licences › Settings, without writing code. Each can be exported as a file, so it can be reviewed like code.

Categories

The company's own list (Design, Engineering, Sales, Data and AI), each with a colour, a default owner, a cost centre and a ledger account. Start from a template or a blank list.

Approval policy for new apps

When a new app needs security review, Finance or a data agreement: by price, by data it touches, by whether it supports SSO. Routes run on Bemmoly's shared approval engine.

Reclaim rules

"No activity for N days", per app or category, with exceptions: people on leave, new joiners, owners' keep list. Ask first or free straight away. A dry run shows what today's rules would take back.

Contract fields

Term, notice period, price per seat, true-up rules, payment method, purchase order and any typed custom field (a DPA date, a reseller), all usable in reminders and filters.

Owners

A business owner and an IT owner per app, with what each gets: renewal reminders, reclaim approvals, requests. Owners can delegate while away, from People.

Connectors

Signals (spend, invoices, sign-ins, browser, devices) and vendor admin APIs for true last-active dates and seat changes. Each is a plug-in with a public interface, so the community can add more.

08How it connects

Licences is its own product with its own app, and a company can run it alone. When other Bemmoly products are installed, they share one sign-in, one person directory and one approval engine through the kernel. A cross-product action opens or calls the other product after the person confirms, and each one has a fallback when that product isn't there.

With other Bemmoly products

  • Expenses: card spend and software claims become discovered apps; moving someone to a company seat asks Expenses to stop their claims. Without it: card statements, bank feeds and the invoices mailbox.
  • Access: SSO sign-ins for use, and seats granted or freed when Licences asks. Without it: Google Workspace or Microsoft Entra connectors, and vendor admin APIs.
  • People: joiners, leavers, leave, teams and cost centres. Without it, the kernel's own people list.
  • Hiring: open roles feed the renewal plan. Without it, a "planned hires" field per team.
  • Assets: installed desktop software per device
  • Helpdesk: "I need an app" requests are handed to Licences; Docs holds the renewal brief as a shared doc

Core objects

  • application: name, category, owners, status (approved, in review, personal, ignored), custom fields
  • subscription: the contract, with term, notice, price, true-up, documents and renewal history
  • seat: a person on an app, role, last active, source of truth (vendor, sign-in)
  • signal: a swipe, invoice, sign-in, grant or domain that points at an app
  • reclaim_run and app_request: the ask, the wait, the result
  • renewal_brief: the plan, the working and who agreed it

10Open questions

  1. Do we ship a browser extension at all?Recommendation: yes, but opt-in per person, off by default, domains only, with a page that shows each person exactly what was sent. Spend and sign-ins find most apps without it.
  2. Who owns a seat, Licences or Access?Recommendation: one seat object in the kernel. Access owns the account and its rights; Licences owns the cost, the count and the reclaim rules. Both read and write the same record.
  3. Should we offer price benchmarks?Recommendation: not from pooled customer data, which a self-hosted product shouldn't collect. Later, an opt-in community dataset of anonymised list prices could be a separate project.
  4. Does Licences talk to vendors?Recommendation: it drafts, a person sends. Notices go from the owner's own mail, so the legal notice is clearly theirs.
  5. Does cloud infrastructure belong here?Recommendation: no. Usage-billed apps show as spend lines only; cloud cost management is its own discipline.
