01The problem today

Most companies can tell you what they paid for their laptops. Far fewer can tell you where each one is today, who has it, and whether it still works well. The facts exist (on the invoice, in device management, in the HR system) but they live in different tools, and the asset register is a spreadsheet someone updates when they remember.

36%of ITAM teams say they have complete visibility of their IT estateFlexera State of ITAM, 2026
71%of HR staff say at least one leaver kept a company laptop or phoneCapterra offboarding survey, 2022
5%of lost or stolen laptops are ever recoveredPonemon Institute for Intel, 2010
The register is a spreadsheet

A quarter of organisations in HDI's study tracked hardware in a spreadsheet, and on average about a quarter of their IT hardware was not inventoried at all. Monitors, docks, badges and furniture are the first to drop off.

HDI, IT hardware asset management, 2015
Leavers keep the laptop

The return is a polite email, then a reminder, then a write-off. Capterra found remote and hybrid leavers were 17% more likely not to return equipment than on-site ones. Nobody owns the chase.

Capterra, 2022
Day one starts without a laptop

HR knows the start date, IT knows the stock, a courier knows the address. Joining them is a thread of messages, and a new hire in another city loses their first morning waiting.

Audits are a clipboard exercise

Someone walks the floor with a printout, ticks tags by eye and types the result back in. Items that moved desks look missing, and items that are actually missing stay on the books.

IT and finance keep different books

IT knows the serial and the holder. Finance knows the cost and the depreciation, in a separate fixed asset sheet. Retirements and disposals rarely reach both, so the two never quite match.

Refresh by age, not by condition

Device management already knows battery health, storage and warranty, but the tools that hold it don't plan purchases. So companies replace every four-year-old laptop, healthy or not, and miss the two-year-old one that is failing.

03Market leaders, and what they teach us

The category splits into five camps: enterprise service platforms, mid-market service desks, device management, discovery and integration platforms, and device-lifecycle services that ship and collect hardware. Below is what each leader does best and where they leave a gap. This is an internal benchmark. Product screens never name these products.

ServiceNow HAMEnterprise

Hardware asset management on a large service platform: stockroom receiving and picking, repair and RMA flows, deploy, swap and retire tasks, and refresh planning from end-of-life data.

The deepest lifecycle model, tied to incidents, changes and the CMDB.
Sold as a subscription on a heavy platform. Rollouts are projects, and small teams are priced out.
FreshserviceMid-market service desk

Asset management inside a friendly service desk: discovery probe and agent, asset types, CSV import, and depreciation by straight line, declining balance or sum of years' digits.

Depreciation and book value on the asset page, set up in admin.
IT-first. Furniture, badges and lab gear fit awkwardly, and joiner logistics are out of scope.
Jamf, Iru (formerly Kandji)Device management

Enrolment, configuration, security and inventory for Apple fleets, with Iru now widening to Windows and Android. They know each device's health in real time.

Zero-touch enrolment: the laptop configures itself on first sign-in.
Only managed devices exist. No cost, no depreciation, no monitors or desks, no people process.
Oomnitza, LansweeperDiscovery and integration

Oomnitza pulls asset data from many systems without an agent and runs workflows on it. Lansweeper scans networks to find every connected device.

Reconciles what each tool says into one record, and finds what nobody registered.
Powerful but technical. Implementation is the hard part, and non-networked things are invisible.
Asset PandaConfigurable tracking

Tracks any physical asset with customisable fields and actions, and a free mobile app that scans QR codes and many barcode types for check-in, check-out and audits.

Anything can be an asset, and the phone is the scanner.
A tracking database more than a lifecycle. No device health, no joiner kits, pricing on request.
Firstbase, WorkwizeDevice-lifecycle services

Buy, ship, store, collect and recycle laptops for distributed teams. Retrieval kits and labels go out automatically when the HR system marks someone as leaving.

The logistics nobody wants to do, done in many countries.
Your asset records live with the service, and the economics tie you to its warehouses.

Open source today means Snipe-IT (check-in and check-out, QR labels, custom fields, EULA acceptance at checkout, requestable assets and audit reminders), GLPI (a service desk with an inventory agent) and Fleet (open-core device management built on osquery). Nobody joins the register to people, finance, the help desk and device health in one premium, self-hosted product. That gap is ours.

04Where Bemmoly does better

We take the leaders' best ideas (the lifecycle model, zero-touch logistics, the phone as scanner) and add what only an open platform that already knows your people can do.

Anything can be an asset, typed your way

A laptop has a serial, memory and a device-management id. A lab oscilloscope has a calibration date. A desk has a floor and a seat. Each type gets its own typed fields, states, depreciation and tag format.

Leaders: most model IT hardware first; furniture and lab gear are an afterthought.
Joiners and leavers run themselves

A start date in People picks a kit, reserves stock and books the courier so the laptop lands before day one. A last day sends a return kit and label, locks the device and closes the loop when it's back.

Leaders: lifecycle services do the logistics, but your records live with them.
Invoice in, assets out

Drop the supplier's invoice and AI reads every line and serial, matches models and the purchase order, creates tagged records and queues the labels. A person checks it before anything is saved.

New: leaders import CSVs or discover devices after they're switched on.
Refresh by condition, not age

Battery health, cycles, storage and warranty come in from device management. AI predicts when each laptop will cross your threshold, so you replace the failing ones and keep the healthy ones a year longer.

Leaders: enterprise tools plan from end-of-life dates; most plan from purchase date.
One register for IT and finance

Cost, depreciation method, book value and disposal live on the same record as the serial and the holder. Retiring a laptop updates the books, and the month's changes export to the ledger.

Leaders: service desks have depreciation; most others leave it to a finance spreadsheet.
Self-service, and no price per asset

Every person sees their devices under "Assigned to me", requests what they need and reports a problem that opens a Helpdesk ticket with diagnostics attached. Track ten assets or a hundred thousand: nothing is metered.

Leaders: priced per asset, per device or per agent, with self-service on higher plans.

05The experience

Eight screens, drawn in Bemmoly's frame and tokens. Click the tabs, or click the highlighted controls inside a screen to follow the flow. The appearance switch at the top right flips every screen between light and dark.

06Make it yours

A design studio, a hospital lab and a 3,000-person sales company own very different things. Nothing about assets is hard-coded. A company changes six things from Assets › Settings, without writing code, and each can be exported as a file so it can be reviewed like code.

Asset types and fields

The company's own types (laptop, oscilloscope, forklift, artwork), each with typed custom fields: text, number, choice, date, person, place, link to another asset. Fields can be filled from a connector and used in views and rules.

Lifecycle states

States and the moves between them, per type. Rules on each move: retiring needs a wipe certificate, assigning a phone needs signed terms, "missing" for 30 days notifies finance.

Depreciation

Straight line, declining balance, sum of years' digits, or none, with useful life, salvage value and the ledger account, set per type and changeable per asset. Multiple books for companies that need tax and accounting views.

Locations and stockrooms

Offices, floors, rooms, desks and stockrooms as a tree, each with an owner, a label printer and a courier. Stock levels and reorder points per stockroom for laptops and consumables.

Tags and labels

Tag formats from tokens (AL-{type}-{seq:4}), label templates sized for your printer, QR, barcode or NFC, and an existing tag kept when you import from another tool.

Kits and request routes

Kits per role and office for joiners, return flows for leavers, what people can request from "Assigned to me", and who approves it, built on the shared workflow and approval engine.

08How it connects

Assets is its own product. It shares the kernel with the others (sign-in, the person directory, the approval engine, custom fields, AI, audit, backups) and calls another product only when it is installed, with the person confirming. Each link below has a fallback for when it is not.

Other Bemmoly products

  • People: holders, managers, offices, desks, and the start dates and last days that trigger kits. Without People, the kernel's person list and a "joining" or "leaving" date set in Assets do the same
  • Helpdesk: "Report a problem" opens a ticket with the device attached, and repairs write back to the timeline. Without it, requests go to Assets' own Repairs list
  • Access: a leaver's devices lock on the last evening, and a missing device is signed out everywhere. Without it, the lock goes through the device-management connector
  • Licences: software found on a laptop counts against seats, and retiring a device frees them. Without it, the software list stays on the asset page
  • Expenses: a laptop bought on a card becomes an asset draft for IT to confirm. Without it, the invoice or a CSV does the job
  • Hiring: an accepted offer reserves the joiner's kit before day one. Without it, the kit starts from the start date
  • Notifications: approvals, returns and "accept your device" reach people by email and in the product, through the kernel

Core objects

  • asset: type, tag, serial, model, state, holder (person or place), location, cost, typed fields
  • asset_type: fields, lifecycle, depreciation rule, tag format, request route
  • asset_event: the timeline, written by Assets and the products it works with (received, assigned, repaired, scanned, retired)
  • location: offices, floors, desks and stockrooms as a tree, with stock levels
  • kit and shipment: joiner and leaver bundles, courier tracking and proof of delivery
  • audit: a scope, the scans, and the reconciliation
  • depreciation_schedule: book value by period, posted to the ledger export

10Open questions

  1. Do we ship our own discovery agent?Recommendation: no. Read from device-management connectors and an osquery-based connector, so we never compete with the tools that already run on every laptop.
  2. Which couriers come first?Recommendation: a generic label connector first (upload a label, paste a tracking number), then one carrier per launch office, chosen by the first companies that adopt it.
  3. Are consumables assets?Recommendation: no tags. Cables, mice and headsets are quantities per stockroom with reorder points, and kits draw them down.
  4. Is Assets the fixed asset register, or a mirror of the ledger?Recommendation: Assets calculates depreciation and posts monthly entries, while the ledger stays the book of record. A mismatch is shown, never silently fixed.
  5. What about personal devices?Recommendation: register them as "personal" with no cost or depreciation, only so Access can check posture. They never appear in audits or refresh plans.
