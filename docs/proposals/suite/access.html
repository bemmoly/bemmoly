Bemmoly Access · product proposal

Access that is ready on day one and gone on the last

Bemmoly Access is a product of its own: use it alone, or with Bemmoly's other products. Each person has one identity. Their apps arrive from their role before their first morning, change when they change teams, and leave, verified, the minute they do. Anyone can ask for more access in a sentence, for exactly as long as they need it. It's open source, it runs on your own server, and it can be your identity provider or sit on top of the directory you already have.

IT and security admins, managers, everyone who signs in Its own product, on Bemmoly's kernel AI optional, local models supported SAML, OIDC, SCIM and passkeys

01The problem today

Every company now runs on a hundred apps, and every one of them is a door. Identity is how attackers get in, and the doors are opened and closed by hand: a ticket to add someone, a checklist to remove them, and a spreadsheet once a year to pretend it was all checked.

22%of breaches began with credential abuse, the most common way inVerizon DBIR, 2025
101apps used by the average company, the first time past 100Okta Businesses at Work, 2025
47%of workers admit using a former employer's password after leavingPasswordManager.com survey, 2023
99.2%less risk of account compromise with multi-factor sign-inMicrosoft Research, 2023
Leavers keep their keys

HR knows someone left on Friday. IT hears about it on Monday, then works through a checklist of apps by hand. Apps outside single sign-on, shared passwords and personal API tokens are the ones that get missed.

Self-reported: nearly half of workers say an old password still worked.
Access only ever grows

When someone changes team they get the new team's apps and keep the old ones. Three moves later an account executive can still open engineering's production console.

Asking is a ticket, and access is forever

People request access in chat, email or a help desk form. Nobody is sure who approves, so it waits. When it's granted, it never ends, even if it was needed for one afternoon.

Access reviews are rubber stamps

Once a quarter a manager gets a spreadsheet of hundreds of rows with no usage data, and approves all of them to make it go away. The audit passes and nothing changes.

Security costs extra

Single sign-on and automatic provisioning are often locked behind an app's enterprise tier, the "SSO tax". Identity suites then charge per person again, with governance sold as a separate licence.

sso.tax has tracked vendors' SSO price jumps since 2018.
HR and IT live in different systems

The person record is in the HR tool, the account is in the directory, and the apps hold their own copies. Starts, transfers and exits are copied between them by people, late and incomplete.

03Market leaders, and what they teach us

The broadest app catalog, and lifecycle that can start from an HR system.

Open source today means Keycloak, Authentik, Zitadel, Authelia and Kanidm. They are good at signing people in: SAML and OIDC, MFA, social and directory login, and in some cases SCIM or a forward-auth proxy for apps with no SSO. None of them knows when someone joins, moves or leaves, and none offers requests, approvals or access reviews. Shared secrets are a neighbouring problem that password managers such as 1Password and Bitwarden solve well; Access does not become a vault, it connects to one so shared credentials are rotated when someone leaves.

04Where Bemmoly does better

We take the best ideas (the catalog, risk-based sign-in, requests in chat, time-bound access) and join them to the one thing identity tools never own: the person record.

The person record drives access

When People records a start date, a transfer or a last day, Access already knows. Joiners, movers and leavers are plans built from that event, not tickets raised by someone who remembered. With People not installed, the kernel's own person list does the same job.

vs Okta and Entra ID: lifecycle reads an HR system through a connector, often on a paid tier.
Removal you can prove

At exit, every account is removed and then checked: the app is asked again, sessions and tokens are listed, and anything that couldn't be automated becomes a named task. The result is an offboarding record with a timestamp per app.

New: leaders deprovision and assume it worked.
Time-bound by default

Sensitive access is granted for hours or days, not forever. Policies by sensitivity decide the approver, the longest duration and whether a fresh passkey check is needed.

vs ConductorOne and Opal: the same idea, without buying a second product on top of your identity provider.
Both modes, one product

Small companies use Access as their identity provider. Companies on Entra ID or Google Workspace keep sign-in there, and Access adds lifecycle, requests and reviews on top. Moving from one mode to the other is a setting, not a migration project.

vs Google Workspace and JumpCloud: governance stops at groups.
AI that reads usage, not just lists

Reviews open with "41 grants were unused for 90 days", the mover's plan flags what they still need for a handover, and requests in plain words map to the right role. It runs on your model, local if you like, and it never grants anything by itself.

vs SailPoint: recommendations, but in a specialist's console.
No SSO tax here, ever

Every person, contractor and service account is included. Single sign-on, provisioning, governance and AI are all in the box, under MIT.

vs every leader: per person per month, with governance as a separate product.

05The experience

Eight screens, drawn in Bemmoly's frame and tokens. Click the tabs, or the highlighted controls inside a screen to follow the flow. The appearance switch at the top right flips every screen between light and dark.

Who sees what. The admin places (People and groups, Requests for the whole company, Access reviews setup, the Audit log) are for IT and security admins. Everyone else opens Access as one place, Applications, which shows their own apps and a box to ask for more. Managers see their approvals and their review decisions, and nothing else. Approvals also arrive in chat and email, so most people never open Access at all.

06Make it yours

Access is open source and used by companies of every shape, so who gets what is never hard-coded. A company sets six things, all from Access › Settings, without writing code. Each can be exported as a file and reviewed like code.

Roles and birthright rules

Sentences that say who gets what by department, team, job, location or employment type ("Engineering in Austin gets the US VPN"). Every change is versioned, with a preview of who gains and loses access before it's live.

Approval policies by sensitivity

Each app or role is Low, Standard, High or Critical (or your own tiers). The tier sets the approvers, the longest time-bound grant, and whether a fresh passkey is needed. Routes run on the kernel's shared approval engine.

Sign-in, session and MFA

Passkeys first, with authenticator codes as a fallback. Session length by sensitivity, managed devices required for chosen apps, and what happens on a risky sign-in: hold, step up or block.

Review cadence

Which apps are reviewed, how often and by whom (manager, app owner or both), what counts as unused, and whether unanswered grants are kept or removed when a review closes.

Directory mode

Bemmoly as the identity provider, or on top of Entra ID, Google Workspace or an LDAP directory. Which side owns groups, how often to sync, and whether Access may write back.

Connectors

SCIM for most apps, API connectors for the rest, a vault connector for shared credentials, and owner tasks for apps with neither. Each connector is a plug-in with a public interface, so the community can add more.

07AI, with a person in charge

Every AI surface follows Bemmoly's rules: lilac marks it, it cites what it read, a person applies the result, and nothing appears while AI is off. In identity, one more rule: AI never grants, removes or approves access by itself.

08How it connects

Access is its own product with its own app, and a company can run it alone. When other Bemmoly products are installed, they share one sign-in, one person directory and one approval engine through the kernel. Access is the one that provides that sign-in when it's the identity provider.

Other Bemmoly products

  • People: starts, transfers, leave and exits are the events that drive lifecycle. Without People, the kernel's own person list and a manual "mark as leaving" do the same
  • Assets: managed devices for sign-in policy, badges by location, and the laptop return at exit. Without Assets, device checks come from a device connector and returns become IT tasks
  • Licences: seats freed when access is removed, costs shown in reviews, and apps found in spend brought under sign-in
  • Helpdesk: "I can't get into…" is answered from the person's access, and requests can start as a ticket
  • Expenses: a leaver's card is frozen, and new software vendors appear as apps to connect
  • Work and Docs: owned projects and documents handed over at exit

Each cross-product step is shown to a person before it runs and calls the other product's public interface. If that product isn't installed, the step becomes a task.

Core objects

  • identity: the person's sign-in, factors, sessions and state, linked to the kernel's person record
  • app and app_account: an application, its protocol, connector and sensitivity, and each person's account in it
  • grant: who has what, its source (birthright, request, group, by hand), approver, expiry and last use
  • role and birthright_rule: rules as data, versioned, with preview results
  • access_request: on the kernel's approval engine
  • lifecycle_plan: a joiner, mover or leaver plan, with per-step verification
  • review_campaign and review_decision
  • signin_event and risk_signal

10Open questions

  1. Build our own identity provider, or wrap an existing open source one?Recommendation: build on the kernel's existing sign-in with well-tested standard libraries for OIDC, SAML and WebAuthn, rather than embedding a separate server. One process, one database, one audit trail. Keep the protocol layer small and fuzz-tested, and publish a conformance report.
  2. When Entra ID or Google Workspace is present, who owns groups?Recommendation: People owns the person, the directory owns sign-in, and Access owns the groups it creates (prefixed and marked). It never edits groups it didn't create unless an admin allows it per group.
  3. What about apps with no SCIM and no API?Recommendation: owner tasks with a due time, a monthly confirmation, and a "verified by" person in the record. Community connectors can replace a task with automation later.
  4. Should Access store app passwords for apps without SSO?Recommendation: no. Being a password vault is a different product with different risks. Connect to the company's vault so shared credentials can be listed and rotated at exit.
  5. How do we avoid locking everyone out?Recommendation: two break-glass accounts outside SSO with hardware keys, alerts on every use, and a signed recovery file made during setup.
  6. Does every employee need to open Access?Recommendation: no. Requests and approvals work from chat and email. The "My apps" place is there for people who want a launcher, and is the only part employees see.
