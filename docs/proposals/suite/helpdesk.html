01The problem today

Every company already has a help desk, usually four or five of them: an IT portal, an HR inbox, a finance spreadsheet, a facilities form and a lawyer's direct messages. People don't know where to ask, so they ask the wrong place, or a colleague, or nobody. The teams on the other side retype what the system already knows.

~$22to resolve one ticket at the service desk, and almost $70 once it reaches desktop supportHDI and MetricNet, 2020
14%of service issues are fully resolved in self-service. Even "very simple" ones: 36%Gartner, 2024

Five front doors, none of them obvious

"Where do I ask about my payslip?" IT has a portal, HR has an email, Finance has a form somewhere. People guess, get bounced, and learn to message the one person they know instead.

The category picker is a guessing game

A 40-item dropdown asks the person to classify their own problem in the help desk's words. They pick wrong, the ticket lands in the wrong queue and waits, and "Other" quietly becomes the biggest category.

Self-service answers, but rarely resolves

Portals and bots point to an article and stop. Even for simple issues most people still end up raising a ticket, often because the content they needed was not there.

Gartner, 2024
Agents retype what the company already knows

Which laptop, which office, who the manager is, which groups they're in, whether they're travelling. Every ticket starts with the same questions, asked back to a person who is already stuck.

Categories and knowledge rot after setup

The taxonomy was designed once, by a consultant or the first admin. Work changes, new problems appear, articles go stale, and nobody notices until the queue tells them, slowly.

AI priced by the answer, people priced by the seat

Leading suites charge per agent per month, then again for every AI resolution. The better the AI gets, the bigger the bill, and HR or Facilities rarely get seats of their own.

03Market leaders, and what they teach us

The category has three camps: customer-support suites that added AI agents, IT and enterprise service management suites that grew into HR and facilities, and AI assistants that sit in front of all of it for employees. This is an internal benchmark. Product screens never name these products.

ZendeskCustomer support · AI agents

AI agents resolve requests across messaging, email, voice and web, detect intent in long threads, take actions in connected systems and hand off to the right team with context. Usage is metered by automated resolutions.

The most complete omnichannel desk, with mature triage and routing.
Built for customers first. Internal desks pay per agent, plus per resolution.
Freshservice and FreshdeskITSM and ESM · Freddy AI

One instance hosts separate workspaces for IT, HR, Finance, Facilities and Legal, each with its own portal. Freddy AI answers employees in chat, creates requests from conversations and assists agents.

Business-team workspaces are the closest thing to "one front door" today.
AI copilot is a paid add-on per agent, and the catalogue is still a form-picker.
ServiceNow ITSM and HRSDEnterprise · Now Assist, Moveworks

The enterprise standard for IT and HR service delivery. Now Assist summarises cases and chats and drafts resolution notes. Its acquisition of Moveworks, completed in December 2025, adds an employee-facing AI front door.

Deep workflows: approvals, fulfilment and HR case privacy done properly.
Long, consultant-led rollouts. Out of reach for most companies under a few thousand people.
Jira Service ManagementIT and dev teams · virtual agent

Request types with their own forms, queues and SLAs, close to the engineering tracker. A virtual service agent answers in chat and raises requests, and Assist brings ticketing into chat tools.

Request types are a clean model: each one owns its form and workflow.
The virtual agent needs a higher plan. The person still has to pick the right request type.
Intercom Fin, HubSpot, Help ScoutCustomer support · AI first

AI agents answer from the help centre and hand off to people. Fin is priced at $0.99 per resolution, HubSpot's customer agent around $0.50. Help Scout drafts replies and summarises threads.

The best "answer first" experience, with clear reporting on what AI resolved.
Made for external customers. "Resolved" can mean the person just stopped replying.
Zammad, osTicket, FreeScout, GLPIOpen source today

Self-hosted ticketing with email, portals, SLAs and knowledge bases. Zammad has added AI ticket summaries and a knowledge base assistant behind a provider of your choice. GLPI pairs tickets with an IT inventory.

Your server, your data, no seat price.
Ticket-first, IT-first and dated to use. None of them is a front door for the whole company.

04Where Bemmoly does better

We take the leaders' best ideas (request types with their own form, the AI front door, answer first) and add what an open, self-hosted family of products can do.

One door, and the person never picks a category

People describe the problem. Helpdesk splits a message with three asks into three items, picks each category with a visible confidence, and asks only when it is unsure.

Leaders: most portals still start with "choose a request type".
Categories that learn from the queue

Each team owns a tree, and each category owns its form, queue, SLA, approval and AI rules. AI watches what didn't fit and proposes new, merged or split categories, with the tickets as evidence.

New: taxonomies elsewhere change only when an admin notices.
Answers that do the thing

Reset MFA, request a monitor, check a reimbursement or a leave balance. Helpdesk calls the right Bemmoly product, shows exactly what will happen, and acts only after the person says yes.

Leaders: employee AI assistants do this, at enterprise prices.
The context is already there

The device, the manager, the office hours, the groups and even the trip someone is on come from the kernel and the other products. Agents stop asking "which laptop?".

New: standalone desks re-import or ask for all of this.
"Resolved" means it stayed resolved

We count an AI answer only when the person confirms it or doesn't come back about it within 3 days, and we show the ones that bounced. No fee rides on the number, so there is no reason to flatter it.

Leaders: per-resolution pricing can count silence as success.
No seat price, for any team

HR, Facilities and Legal get their own desks with no agent licence and no paid AI tier. AI runs on the workspace's chosen model, including a local one.

Leaders: per agent per month, plus AI add-ons or resolution fees.

05The experience

Seven screens, drawn in Bemmoly's tokens, in Helpdesk's own shell. Click the tabs, or the highlighted controls inside a screen to follow the flow. The appearance switch at the top right flips every screen between light and dark.

06Make it yours

The owner's question: "There might be different categories. How do we manage those categories?" The answer is that a category is the unit of configuration. Every team owns a tree, and every category in it owns six things. Children inherit from their parent and override only what differs, so a 100-category desk is still easy to reason about. Everything lives in Helpdesk › Settings, without code, and each team's setup exports as a file that can be reviewed like code.

Teams and queues

A team (IT, People, Finance, Facilities, Legal, or any you add) has members, queues per office or skill, its own portal section and its own privacy. People cases can be hidden from every other admin.

Categories and forms

A tree per team, as deep as needed. Each category has its intake form built from typed custom fields, its visibility, and the words people use for it, which help AI place new tickets. Templates for IT, HR and Facilities to start.

Routing rules

Sentences built from blocks: when the category is, the office is, the person is new, VIP or travelling, then send to a queue, assign round robin or by skill, set a priority. Every rule can be replayed against last month.

SLAs and business hours

SLA policies by priority and category, timed in the requester's or the team's business hours. Hours per office, with holidays from People when it's installed. Pause while waiting on the person.

Channels and approvals

Portal, email addresses per team, chat connectors, the palette and phone. Approvals use Bemmoly's shared workflow engine: a manager tap, an app owner, a cost threshold, or a chain.

Macros and AI actions

Saved replies with fields, shared per team. Per category, what AI may do: answer from articles, fill the form, act in another product after confirmation, or nothing. Plus the confidence it needs to auto-categorise.

07AI, with a person in charge

Every AI surface follows Bemmoly's rules: lilac marks it, it cites its sources, a person confirms anything that changes something, and nothing lilac appears while AI is off. The model is the workspace's choice, including a local one. Helpdesk works fully without AI: the same categories, forms, routing and SLAs, placed by keywords and rules.

08How it connects

Helpdesk is its own product. Use it alone, or with the others. It shares Bemmoly's kernel: one sign-in, one person directory, the workflow and approval engine, typed custom fields, the AI layer, audit and backups. Other products add actions and context; each action opens or calls that product with the person's confirmation, and each has a fallback when that product isn't installed.

Other Bemmoly products

  • Access: MFA resets, password resets and app access requests. Without it: a ticket to IT with the right form
  • Assets: request a monitor, see the person's devices, push an update. Without it: an IT task and a free-text device field
  • People: leave balances, managers, offices, holidays, onboarding plans. Without it: the kernel's people list and a People team article
  • Expenses: "has my claim been paid?" answered from the claim, trips as context. Without it: routed to Finance
  • Licences: "I need a seat" becomes a licence request with the cost shown. Without it: an IT software request
  • Work and Docs: incidents link to engineering work items; articles can live as Docs pages

Core objects

  • request: one ask, with requester, category, form values, channel, priority, SLA clocks, state and the items it was split from
  • team, queue, category: the tree, each category with form, routing, SLA, approval and AI policy
  • article: knowledge with owner, audience, language and the tickets it came from
  • incident: a cause linking many requests, with one shared reply
  • ai_action: what AI proposed, what it read, who confirmed and what changed

10Open questions

  1. External customer support: same product or a separate one?Recommendation: the same product and engine, with an "external desk" that has its own portal, branding, contacts and stricter AI sources. Internal and external tickets never share a queue. Ship it after the internal desk is proven.
  2. May AI act without confirmation for low-risk actions?Recommendation: reads (a balance, a status) need no confirmation; anything that changes something always asks. Admins can't remove the confirmation, only the action.
  3. Should People cases live in Helpdesk or in People?Recommendation: in Helpdesk, as a private team with its own visibility, so there is one front door. People keeps the employee record and links to cases.
  4. Who owns the category tree when teams overlap (a laptop for a new hire)?Recommendation: one owning team per category, plus linked tasks for others. Cross-team categories appear in both trees but are edited by the owner.
  5. Which chat tools come first?Recommendation: email, portal and palette in the first release; Slack and Microsoft Teams as connectors next, built on the public connector interface so the community can add others.
