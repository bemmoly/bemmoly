01The problem today

Most companies still run performance as a twice-a-year writing exercise. Managers rebuild six months from memory, people write self reviews from a blank page, and the ratings say almost as much about the person giving them as the person receiving them. The evidence exists, in the issue tracker, the docs and the support queue. The review never sees it.

14%of employees strongly agree their reviews inspire them to improveGallup, 2019
~2M hoursa year Deloitte found it spent on forms, meetings and ratingsDeloitte, in HBR, 2015
62%of the variance in ratings came from the rater, not the person rated (53% in a second sample)Scullen, Mount and Goff, 2000
22%more feedback about personality for women than for men, in written reviewsTextio, 2022
Reviews are written from memory

The last six weeks crowd out the first five months. What someone shipped in April, the incident they calmed in June, the doc everyone still links to: none of it is in the form unless someone remembers to dig it up.

The blank self review

People spend an evening scrolling old tickets to remember what they did, then undersell it or oversell it. The ones who are good at writing about themselves do better, whatever their work was like.

Goals are set in January and stale by March

Goals live in a slide or a spreadsheet, with progress typed in by hand before each review. Nobody can see how a team goal connects to the company's, so alignment is a meeting, not a fact.

1:1s turn into status meetings

No agenda, notes in a private doc, actions forgotten by the next one. Only 16% of employees say their last conversation with their manager was extremely meaningful.

Gallup, nearly 15,000 employees
Ratings measure the rater

Some managers rate high, some rate low, and vague or personality-based language creeps in unevenly. Calibration happens in a spreadsheet, and the reason someone's rating moved is lost when the meeting ends.

Scullen et al. 2000 · Textio 2022
One process, priced per person

Tools assume an annual cycle with a five-point scale and charge per person per month, with AI sold as an add-on. A 40-person startup with no ratings and a 4,000-person company with a ladder per job family both have to bend.

03Market leaders, and what they teach us

The category has moved from annual appraisals to continuous performance: goals, check-ins, 1:1s and feedback between reviews, and since 2024 AI that drafts and summarises. Below is what each leader does best and where they leave a gap. Lattice here means the HR software company at lattice.com, not Bemmoly's former code name. This is an internal benchmark. Product screens never name these products.

LatticePeople platform · US

Reviews, goals, 1:1s, feedback, engagement and pay in one product. Its AI agent drafts reviews from 1:1s, past reviews, feedback and growth areas, showing its sources, and summarises review packets during calibration.

The most complete suite, with AI that shows where each line came from and leaves ratings to managers.
Its evidence is what people typed into it. The work itself lives in other tools. Cloud only, per person.
Culture AmpEngagement-led · AU

Known for survey science. Perform adds reviews, goals, 1:1s and calibration, with a ratings preview where calibrators mark "I support" or "I have questions" before the meeting.

Its AI is strongest on survey comments. On the performance side it was still arriving in 2025.
15FiveManager-led · US

The check-in habit, and drafting from the whole period to fight recency bias.
Kona is a paid add-on, and it works by joining and listening to the meeting.
LeapsomeAll-in-one · DE

Reviews of every kind, OKRs with goal trees, competency frameworks that drive reviews and learning paths, meetings with AI notes and follow-ups.

Competencies tied to reviews and learning, and goal trees that show who owns what.
The breadth can overwhelm a small team. Modules are priced separately.
BetterworksGoal-led enterprise · US

An enterprise rollout. Lighter on the 1:1 and the day-to-day.
WorkdayHR suite

Performance, check-ins, feedback, goals and talent reviews inside the HR system of record. AI agents that draft data-backed reviews and check-ins were announced in 2025.

One record for the person, from hire to promotion to pay.
Heavy to configure, and at its best only if the company runs all of it.

Open source today means Odoo Appraisals (manager and self evaluations from question templates), Frappe HR (appraisal cycles with weighted KRAs per designation, 360 feedback and a computed final score), and OrangeHRM (KPIs per job title, reviews and OKRs). All three are forms inside a larger HR system: useful for recording an appraisal, with no continuous loop of check-ins and 1:1s, no evidence from the work, and no AI. Nobody offers a premium, self-hosted performance product. That gap is ours.

On practice. Google's re:Work guide grades key results from 0.0 to 1.0, puts the sweet spot at 0.6 to 0.7, keeps OKRs public, and says plainly that OKRs are not employee evaluations. Bemmoly follows that: goal scores appear in a review as context, never as a formula for a rating.

04Where Bemmoly does better

We take the leaders' best ideas (the weekly habit, goal trees, sourced AI drafts, bias nudges) and add what only an open, self-hosted platform next to the work can do.

Evidence from the work itself

The evidence brief cites shipped epics, merged design docs, incident write-ups, Helpdesk resolutions, interviews given and kudos received. Every line opens the real thing. People write the review; the brief makes sure nothing is forgotten.

vs leaders: AI drafts from what was typed into the performance tool (1:1 notes, feedback, past reviews). The work stays in other tools.
Goals that update themselves

A key result can be a query in Work ("issues in epic PLAT-88"), a metric from a connector, or a number someone owns. Check-ins add a confidence call, so the goal tree shows both progress and how people feel about it.

vs leaders: goal-led tools offer integrations, but most goals are still updated by hand before the review.
AI never rates a person

A product rule, not a setting. AI gathers, summarises, cites and points out vague language. It never suggests a rating, a score, a ranking or a "flight risk" for anyone. In calibration it looks at the process (spread by manager, evidence by office), not at people.

vs leaders: they keep managers in control of ratings too. We make it visible on every screen and impossible to switch on.
1:1s prepared, not recorded

Both people add to a shared agenda. Talking points come from the past two weeks of work and check-ins, so the meeting starts in the right place. Nothing listens in. Notes are written by the two people in the room.

vs leaders: the newest 1:1 AI joins the call to transcribe it. Ours prepares the meeting instead.
Fair by design

Peer anonymity with a minimum number of responses, nudges on vague, personality-based and absolute words, a recency check against the brief, and a recorded reason for every calibration change, visible to the person's manager and the People team.

vs leaders: Betterworks flags biased words. Few check recency against the record, or require a reason for every calibration move.
Your process, no seat price

Annual, half-yearly, quarterly or project cycles. Five points, three points, or no ratings at all. A ladder per job family. All in settings, exportable as files, self-hosted, with AI on the model you choose or off.

vs leaders: per person per month, with AI and calibration in higher tiers or add-ons.

05The experience

Eight screens, drawn in Bemmoly's frame and tokens. Click the tabs, or click the highlighted controls inside a screen to follow the flow. The appearance switch at the top right flips every screen between light and dark.

06Make it yours

Bemmoly is open source and used by companies of every shape, so no part of the performance process is hard-coded. A 30-person startup with no ratings and a 3,000-person company with a ladder per job family both set it up from Performance › Settings, without writing code. Every setting can be exported as a file and reviewed like code.

Cycles

Templates for annual, half-yearly, quarterly, project-based and 90-day probation reviews. Each cycle picks its steps (self, peer, upward, manager, calibration, sign-off), dates, and who takes part by a filter on the person record.

Goals

A method per team: OKRs scored 0 to 1, KPIs with targets, or simple goals with a done date. Quarterly or custom periods, whether goals must align upward, and which ones are private.

Competencies and levels

A ladder per job family, with levels and expectations in plain words. Competencies can be weighted per level, shared across families, or used only for growth and never for ratings.

Ratings, or none

Five points, three points, separate "what" and "how" ratings, or no ratings at all, just written reviews. Labels are the company's own. Distribution guidance is optional, and never a quota.

Feedback and who sees what

Peer feedback named, anonymous to the person, or fully anonymous, with a minimum number of responses. A matrix for who sees each part of a review, and when results are shared and signed.

Evidence and AI

Which sources the brief may read, and a list it never reads (leave, health, pay, private notes). The model is the workspace's choice, including a local one, and AI can be off for one cycle or for good.

07AI, with a person in charge

Every AI surface follows Bemmoly's rules: lilac marks it, it cites its sources, a person applies the result, and nothing lilac appears while AI is off. In Performance there is one more rule, fixed in the product: AI never rates, scores or ranks a person. The model is the workspace's choice, from the models.dev catalog or a local model.

Privacy. Performance data is among the most sensitive a company holds. The brief never reads leave, health, pay, private 1:1 notes, check-in mood or a person's own Helpdesk requests. A workspace can run every performance feature on a local model, or turn AI off for one cycle. Nothing is used to train a model. Briefs are not stored as scores, and every brief is visible to the person it is about.

08How it connects

Its own product. Use it alone, or with the others. Underneath it shares Bemmoly's kernel: one sign-in across products, one person directory, the shared workflow engine for sign-offs, typed custom fields, the AI layer, audit and backups. Cross-product actions open or call the other product with the person's confirmation, and each one has a fallback when that product isn't installed.

Other Bemmoly products

  • Work: epics, issues and metrics drive key results, and closed work fills check-ins and the brief. Without Work, a tracker connector does the same, or progress is typed by hand
  • Docs: design docs and postmortems count as evidence, and a finished review can be exported as a doc. Without it, people attach links
  • People: manager, level, job family, start dates and time off. Promotions write back to the person record. Without it, the kernel's own people list holds managers and levels
  • Helpdesk: resolutions for support staff, incident counts for team goals. Without it, these sources are simply absent from the brief
  • Hiring: interviews given count as contribution, and ladders double as hiring levels. Without it, not shown
  • Time and Expenses: never read for reviews. Hours worked is not performance

Core objects

  • goal and key_result: owner, period, method, parent goal, and a progress source (manual, work query, metric connector)
  • check_in: week, items picked, confidence per goal, blockers, mood (private trend only)
  • one_on_one and agenda_item: shared and private notes, actions that carry over
  • review_cycle, review, evidence_item: each item points at its source object, so citations never break
  • calibration_session and rating_change: from, to, who agreed, and the reason
  • ladder, competency, level, growth_plan, kudos

10Open questions

  1. Should the brief show counts, like issues closed?Recommendation: only as context inside a person's own brief, never ranked, compared between people or shown on a dashboard. Counting issues rewards splitting them.
  2. Do we ever record 1:1s?Recommendation: not in the first releases. If added later, both people opt in for each meeting, transcripts stay with the two of them, and a local model is the default.
  3. Are kudos public by default?Recommendation: public to the team, with a private option. Kudos feed the brief as evidence, never as a score or a count in calibration.
  4. Does compensation live here?Recommendation: no. Calibrated outcomes are handed to People for pay reviews, through the shared workflow engine. Pay never appears in Performance.
  5. Can a person ask to remove an item from their brief?Recommendation: yes. They can hide any item from their own brief and the manager sees that it was hidden, not what it was. The source object is untouched.
